Distributions, latent variables
and the VAE

Notation

x: observed outputz: a per-example latent drawε: fixed-distribution noise
θ: decoder / generatorφ: encoderψ: discriminator

p(z) is the prior. pθ(x|z) is the decoder likelihood. pθ(z|x) is the true posterior. qφ(z|x) is its learned approximation.

KL measures expected extra surprisal

KL(P ∥ Q) = Σᵢ Pᵢ log(Pᵢ/Qᵢ)
H(P,Q) = H(P) + KL(P ∥ Q)

Natural logs give nats. P weights the expectation. KL is nonnegative but asymmetric. A zero-weight term contributes zero; positive P against zero Q gives infinity.

For P=(0.8,0.2), Q=(0.6,0.4): KL(P∥Q)=0.091516222; reverse KL=0.104649629. A negative individual contribution is possible.

A model and a bound

pθ(x,z) = p(z) pθ(x|z)
pθ(x) = ∫ p(z) pθ(x|z) dz
ELBO = E_q[log pθ(x|z)] − KL(qφ(z|x) ∥ p(z))
log pθ(x) = ELBO + KL(qφ(z|x) ∥ pθ(z|x))

The loss has a prior KL. The gap has a true-posterior KL. Jensen's inequality uses concavity of log. An exact expectation is a lower bound; one Monte Carlo realisation need not be.

Reparameterize, then differentiate

std = exp(0.5 * logvar)
z = mu + std * epsilon       # epsilon ~ N(0,I)
KL = 0.5 * Σⱼ(muⱼ² + exp(logvarⱼ) − 1 − logvarⱼ)

For μ=1, σ=0.5, ε=−0.4 and f(z)=z²: z=0.8, f=0.64, ∂f/∂μ=1.6, ∂f/∂σ=−0.64, ∂f/∂logvar=−0.16. Keep ε fixed for finite differences.

Train and generate

For binary observations: use BCE with raw logits, sum over pixels; sum KL over latent coordinates; add per example, then average the batch. β=1 is the vanilla negative ELBO.

Generation: prior → z → decoder → observation distribution. The encoder is unnecessary. A probability image is different from a Bernoulli draw. A uniform latent grid is different from prior sampling.

GAN updates
and model comparison

One label convention

D(x) is the displayed probability that x is real. Code uses raw logits a(x). Real target: 1. Fake target for D: 0. Fake target for G: 1.

L_D = −E_real log D(x) − E_z log(1 − D(G(z)))
L_G = −E_z log D(G(z))        # non-saturating

At D(real)=0.8 and D(fake)=0.3: L_D=0.579818495 and L_G=1.203972804.

One complete alternating iteration

# D phase
D.requires_grad_(True)
opt_D.zero_grad(); opt_G.zero_grad()
fake = G(z)
loss_D = BCE_logits(D(real), ones)
loss_D += BCE_logits(D(fake.detach()), zeros)
loss_D.backward(); opt_D.step()

# G phase: fresh graph through updated D
opt_D.zero_grad(); opt_G.zero_grad()
D.requires_grad_(False)
fake = G(new_z)
loss_G = BCE_logits(D(fake), ones)
loss_G.backward(); opt_G.step()
D.requires_grad_(True)

Freezing D parameters preserves the input derivative. Detaching fake or using no_grad around D in the G phase removes the needed path. The minimal models have no BatchNorm/Dropout state.

Why the practical generator objective?

With D=sigmoid(a), the minimax derivative is −D; the non-saturating derivative is D−1. At D=0.01 they are −0.01 and −0.99. The complete parameter gradient also includes da/dx and dx/dθ.

What a loss curve cannot tell you

Inspect fixed-seed samples, diversity and coverage. Attractive samples may miss a mode. D≈1/2 is not proof of matching distributions. Generated scientific images and signals are not measurements. The supplied glyph and 2D runs are controlled teaching examples.
