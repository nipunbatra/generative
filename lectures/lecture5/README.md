# Lecture 5: Autoencoders and the VAE, from the ELBO

A main route of 67 slides (about 92 minutes) in six parts and twenty-five numbered sections, with nine optional slides after it. It is a condensed version of Lecture 2 (its generation story, autoencoders and the VAE) for a class that has already seen Lecture 1's ELBO. Nothing from Lecture 2 was removed: Lecture 2 remains the full two-session route. A 60-minute cut is listed in the speaker notes of the contents slide (and below).

The story: generative models are everywhere, and they share one recipe (random numbers in, a learned network, a sample out). Before the uses, the names: x is the data and y a label, a classifier learns p(y | x) and a generative model p(x), drawn on one number per image (its ink) so that p(x), p(x, y) and p(y | x) are curves tied by Bayes’ rule. An autoencoder squeezes a digit into 2 numbers and rebuilds it; watching its codes during training shows clusters forming at once and then drifting outward, and an autoencoder trained on only 200 digits overfits, its codes flying apart. But its decoder cannot turn random numbers into digits; sampling the real codes instead works in 2 numbers, but it takes a second model fitted after training (as Stable Diffusion does at scale). Three fixes, one at a time, turn it into a VAE: choose where codes live (N(0, I)), send each image to a small cloud of codes, and pull every cloud towards N(0, I) with a KL. The fixes give a loss; the next part shows why it is the right one. It opens with one picture: Lecture 1's problem and the VAE drawn the same way (an unknown with a prior, data, a likelihood, a posterior out of reach because of the evidence, and a q), with the three differences marked (one code per image, a decoder learned too, one encoder for every q). It then tells the model's story from z: a code from the prior, an image from the likelihood; then runs it backwards to the posterior, shows why the evidence is out of reach, and picks the closest Gaussian, the variational approximation, by its ELBO. Then it derives the loss: start from Lecture 1's ELBO with the names swapped, choose Gaussian pixels (noise 0.2) and a Gaussian prior and q, and minus the ELBO is Part 4's loss, rebuild + KL, with its weight decided; nothing is guessed. The last part puts all the maths on one page and traces every term of the loss back to KL and the ELBO. Lecture 1's colours carry over (q purple, likelihood orange, prior green, evidence blue, posterior teal, KL to the posterior red), and loss slides show the target x in teal, the prediction x̂ in purple and the loss in red. Slides that rely on Lecture 1 link back to it (↗ Lecture 1, section).

**Running example.** Test digit #90 of MNIST, a 3, followed through the autoencoder (its loss pixel by pixel), the posterior over codes, per-image variational inference, the encoder's q, and one VAE training step.

**Live figures.** Autoencoder training replayed, then carried on to 1,000 epochs; the codes during training, with all 60,000 digits and with 200 (overfitting); hover maps of the codes; two code explorers (the autoencoder, then the VAE); Lecture 1's VI fitting q for our 3 (press play); an ELBO explorer, new here, where you move q for our 3 and watch the reconstruction term, the KL to the prior and the ELBO against log p(x) (Lecture 2's decoder runs in the browser, with 8 × 8 Gauss–Hermite averages that match the notebook to about 0.1); and seven trained VAEs with β from 0 to 256.

## Use

Open `index.html` through the series HTTP server (`python3 -m http.server` in `lectures/`). Press F to present full screen. Questions, expected answers and transitions are in the speaker notes (press N).

The deck reuses Lecture 2's data, examples and live figures: `content.js` imports `../lecture2/widgets.js` and loads `../lecture2/data/science.json`, `digits.json`, `nets.json` and the generated examples in `../lecture2/assets/`. `widgets.js` holds this lecture's own ELBO explorer. Every number on the slides was computed by Lecture 2's pipeline or, for the explorer, by Lecture 2's decoder in the browser; nothing here is retrained.

The notebook `lecture5-autoencoders-vae.ipynb` has saved outputs and the same section numbers as the slides; every slide's ▶ Notebook link opens its section in Colab. It loads Lecture 2's trained networks from `../lecture2/models/` (in Colab it downloads them and MNIST) and trains only a few small models, so it runs in about three minutes. Section 1.3 trains the classifier and draws the ink curves, Section 2.4 repeats the overfitting run on 200 digits exactly, Section 5.2 scores three codes against our 3, Section 5.3 estimates the evidence by brute force, and Sections 4.1–4.3 run the three fixes (N(0, I) draws, the reparameterisation trick, the KL penalties), Section 5.4 repeats Lecture 1's variational inference on our 3 and compares four Gaussians q, Section 5.5 computes the ELBO from its definition with torch.distributions densities and gets Part 4's loss minus a constant, Section 5.6 computes the ELBO explorer's three presets, and Section 6.1 computes every term of the loss for our 3 from scratch.

## Rebuild

From `lectures/`, with a Python that has torch, torchvision, nbclient and matplotlib, and MNIST in `$MNIST_ROOT/MNIST/raw/`:

```sh
python lecture5/scripts/epochs.py           # training replayed to 1,000 epochs: data/epochs.json (about 15 minutes)
python lecture5/scripts/xy.py               # p(y | x) from a classifier, and the ink curves: data/xy.json (seconds)
python lecture5/scripts/empirical.py        # codes sampled from the real ones: data/empirical.json (seconds)
python lecture5/scripts/build_notebook.py   # executes the notebook and writes data/notebook-anchors.json
node scripts/verify.mjs 5                    # every state, layout checks, speaker-notes.md, manifest.json
node scripts/export.mjs 5                    # exports/completed.pdf and exports/all-builds.pdf
```

`epochs.py` replays Lecture 2's training of its 2-number autoencoder step for step, so its epoch-30 codes and rebuilds are Lecture 2's; that needs the device Lecture 2 trained on (mps). If Lecture 2's data or networks change (`lecture2/scripts/train.py`, `science.py`), re-run all four commands.
