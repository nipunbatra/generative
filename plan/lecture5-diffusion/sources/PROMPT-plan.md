- Lecture 5 of a five-lecture generative-modelling block. Students have just had: Lecture 1 KL divergence (built from information theory: surprise, entropy, Huffman codes, cross-entropy), variational inference, the ELBO derived one rule per slide, the reparameterisation trick, closed-form Gaussian KL (they derived KL between two Gaussians step by step), Monte Carlo estimates; running example a tiny Bayesian neural network. Lecture 3 VAEs (prior, decoder likelihood, amortised encoder, ELBO = reconstruction - KL, reparameterisation, samples are blurry). Lecture 4 GANs (discriminator signal, alternating updates, mode collapse, no likelihood).
- Format: an HTML slide deck with numbered parts and sections (e.g. "2.3 The shortcut"), progressive reveals (builds), a story line in a bottom bar per build, KaTeX maths, speaker notes with ask / expected answer / transition, a "Notebook" link on every slide opening the matching cell of a companion Colab notebook, and live interactive figures (sliders, play buttons, hover) driven by recorded runs of real trained models or by exact formulas computed in the browser. ## The instructor's teaching standard (from his feedback on earlier lectures)
- Quality bar: Lilian Weng's blog, Jay Alammar's illustrated explanations, 3Blue1Brown; his own PML 2023 lectures, which "earn each tool through a failure the students have just watched, predict before revealing, and tie equations, code and plots together". He complained earlier decks had "no story".
- Slow, intuitive, example-driven; one move per click; a running example with real numbers after every equation; derive, never assert ("show hard things completely step by step"); ask the students, reveal the answer on the next click.
- Each part opens with what we are trying to do and why, and closes with a recap that leads into the next part. Introduce a concept only when it is needed (forward references confuse).
- Mechanism figures, not boxes of text. Minimal annotated PyTorch code whenever an idea becomes an algorithm. Credit sources on the slide that uses them. Caveats in speaker notes.
- He loved following one excellent video end to end and going slower than it (for KL he followed Jia-Bin Huang's video and asked for each idea to be explained in its own slide).
