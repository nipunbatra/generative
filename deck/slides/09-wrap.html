Three ways to generate

Same goal, three different training signals

Autoregressive: exact likelihood, product of next-token probabilities; slow sampling. VAE: explicit latent model, trained on a lower bound; tends to blur. GAN: no likelihood at all, trained against a learned judge; sharp, can miss modes and is finicky to train.

None is "the best". Modern image models (after the GAN lecture) took a fourth route.

Before you leave

Three exit questions

  1. To generate a new digit with a trained VAE, which parts of the network do you use? The prior and the decoder only: $z\sim\N(0, I)$, then decode. The encoder is for training.
  2. Two different KL divergences appear in the VAE story. What does each compare, and which one is in the loss? $\KL(q\,\|\,\text{prior})$ is in the loss ({{evidence.kl_prior}} for our 3). $\KL(q\,\|\,\text{true posterior})$ is the gap between the ELBO and $\log p_\theta(x)$ ({{vi.gap_enc}}).
  3. We never compute $p_\theta(x)$. Why does maximising the ELBO still pull $q$ towards the true posterior? $\log p_\theta(x) = \ELBO + \KL(q\,\|\,\text{posterior})$, and the left side does not depend on $q$. Whatever the ELBO gains, the KL loses.

Appendix

A second route to the ELBO

Part 5 reached the ELBO from $\KL(q\,\|\,\text{posterior})$. Many textbooks reach it from $\log p_\theta(x)$ with Jensen's inequality instead. Same bound, one more tool.

Appendix · the ELBO through Jensen · move 1 of 4

Start from what we want

$$\log p_\theta(x) = \log \int p_\theta(x,z)\,dz$$
Maximum likelihood wants this number large for our 3, and for every image in the data. The integral over all codes is the part we cannot compute.

A second route to the bound of Part 5, starting from $\log p_\theta(x)$ instead of the KL.

Recall p(x, z) = p(z) p(x | z): prior times decoder. Each of the next slides makes exactly one change. Ask the class to name the move before you click.

Appendix · the ELBO through Jensen · move 2 of 4

Multiply and divide by the encoder's cloud

$$\log \int p_\theta(x,z)\,dz$$
$$= \log \int \hl{q_\varphi(z\mid x)}\;\frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{\hl{q_\varphi(z\mid x)}}\,dz$$
Multiplying and dividing by the same positive number changes nothing. We are free to choose any $q$; we choose the encoder's Gaussian, which is positive everywhere.

Why bother? Because the next move turns this integral into an average under $q$, and $q$ knows where the patch is.

Appendix · the ELBO through Jensen · move 3 of 4

Read the integral as an average under $q$

$$\log \int q_\varphi(z\mid x)\,\frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{q_\varphi(z\mid x)}\,dz$$
$$= \log\; \hl{\E_{z\sim q_\varphi(z\mid x)}}\!\left[\frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{q_\varphi(z\mid x)}\right]$$
From the $\E$ slide: $\int q(z)\,f(z)\,dz = \E_{z\sim q}[f(z)]$. Here $f(z)$ is the ratio $p_\theta(x,z)/q_\varphi(z\mid x)$: one number for each code.

Still exact. We now average over codes drawn from the encoder's cloud, right where the patch is.

Appendix · the ELBO through Jensen · a tool for move 4

Jensen's inequality, in one picture

$\log$ curves downwards (it is concave), so every chord lies below the curve.

Two values, $R=1$ and $R=4$:

$$\underbrace{\log 2.5}_{\log \text{ of the average}} = 0.916 \;\ge\; \underbrace{\tfrac12(0 + 1.386)}_{\text{average of the logs}} = 0.693$$
$$\log \E[R] \;\ge\; \E[\log R]$$

Read the picture: two orange points on the curve; their chord lies below the curve. The midpoint of the chord (average of logs) sits below the curve at the midpoint (log of the average).

Appendix · the ELBO through Jensen · move 4 of 4

Swap the log and the average: the only inequality

$$\log p_\theta(x) = \log\,\E_{q}\!\left[\frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{q_\varphi(z\mid x)}\right]$$
$$\log p_\theta(x) \;\hl{\ge}\; \E_{q}\!\left[\log\frac{p_\theta(x,z)}{q_\varphi(z\mid x)}\right] \;=:\; \ELBO$$
Jensen with $R = p_\theta(x,z)/q_\varphi(z\mid x)$. The right side is the Evidence Lower BOund: it can never exceed $\log p_\theta(x)$.

The same ELBO as Part 5. This route shows it is a bound; Part 5's route also shows the gap is $\KL(q\,\|\,\text{posterior})$.
